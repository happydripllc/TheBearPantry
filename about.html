Our Story

Meet the Bears

Every jar starts the same way it always has — around a family table with too many people at it.

Mama Bear

The woman who feeds everybody.

She'll feed you before she learns your name.

Mama Bear has been an adopted mom, grandmother, and unofficial feeder-of-anyone-who-walks-through-the-door for as long as her actual family can remember. Neighbors showed up for one thing and left with a plate, a jar, and instructions to come back for more.

She's never really stopped cooking, canning, or coming up with new things to put in a jar. Somewhere along the way, "just take some home with you" turned into people actually asking to buy it — and Mama Bear, who has never once said no to anybody, finally said yes to that too.

Papa Bear

The one with the cowboy hat.

Papa Bear was the steady, warm, quietly-in-charge anchor of our world. He knew how to do just about everything, and he was always eager to teach anyone who asked. He was the first to step up whenever someone was in need, loving everyone around him completely, without restraint or expectations.

He didn't cook a day in his life — why would he, when he was utterly devoted to Mama Bear's cooking? He deeply appreciated good food, though he had zero tolerance for spice (or injustice). He held sweet, strong opinions on everything from how green beans should be canned to who was allowed a second helping (everybody, always).

Papa Bear passed away in August 2024. This business isn't a solemn memorial (he'd hate that), but his generous spirit is woven into every corner of this pantry. His name rests proudly on the recipes he adored, his favorite hat sits right on the bear in our logo, and the things he built with his own hands are still loved by the people he cherished most.

How The Bear Pantry Started

It was never supposed to be a business.

It started the way most good things in this family start — somebody said "you should sell this," and everybody agreed, and Mama Bear ignored them for about two years. Jars kept leaving the house anyway. Friends started asking ahead of time. Neighbors started requesting flavors.

Eventually the math stopped making sense any other way, and The Bear Pantry became a real, legal, small Texas home-based business — built under the state's cottage food rules, made in the same kitchen it always was.

Nothing about how it's made changed. There's just a name on the jar now.

The Whole Crew

There's always more family than chairs.

Kids, grandkids, in-laws, and the neighbors who may as well be — this pantry gets help, opinions, and taste-testers from all of them.

Still cooking. Still canning. Still setting an extra place.

Mama Bear is still in that kitchen most days, probably preserving something nobody realized needed preserving. Papa Bear's name is still on the jars he'd be proudest of. And the table — the whole reason any of this exists — still has room for one more.
